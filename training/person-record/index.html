01

One record

One person. One record. For life.

A person record is one human being's page in Slate. It stays with them for their whole relationship with Gateway: from the first form they fill out, through applying, studying, graduating, and maybe coming back for another degree years later.

  1. 1
    Exactly one per person

    Every person gets one record. Think of it as a file folder with their name on the tab.

  2. 2
    It grows over time

    More and more papers go into the folder over the years, but it's still one folder. Jordan's has six years of history and three applications in it.

  3. 3
    Two records means half a story

    If Jordan has two records, half of his history is on each and nobody sees the whole picture. That's why the first habit of Slate is search before you create.

Sources

Most records create themselves

People come in through a handful of front doors. Each time, Slate checks whether that person is already in the system, by things like email and name. If they are, their record is updated. If not, a new one is created.

When matching misses

Two records, one human

A new email address or a nickname can slip past matching and create a duplicate. Whoever opens the second record thinks Jordan is brand new. Merging puts the whole story back on one record.

05

Habits

Keeping the record healthy

Records create themselves. Our job is to keep them accurate, so the next person who opens one can trust what they see.

Search before you create

Try name, email, and phone before making a new record.

Leave a note

After any meaningful conversation. Short and specific.

Fix it or flag it

Bounced email? Misspelled name? Correct it or tell someone who can.

Facts in the right place

Person facts on the person. Program, term, and decision on the application.

Watch for duplicates

Two records for one human? Report it so they can be merged.

Vague note

“Talked to Jordan.”

Next month, nobody knows what was said or what to do next.
