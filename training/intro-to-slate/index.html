The right questions

Where? What? When?

Where are the right people? What are the right classes? When is the right time? Every enrollment conversation is trying to answer these three.

Where

Where are the right people?

  • Do the right people know we exist?
  • Do we know where to go to tell them about Gateway?

What

What are the right classes?

  • What are their interests?
  • What have they taken already?
  • What is their career or ministry goal?

When

When is the right time?

  • Are they old enough?
  • Are they in a bachelor's degree?
  • Are they ready for seminary?

Two sides of the coin

A good conversation needs both sides

You bring what you know about Gateway. They bring what only they know about their own life. Flip the coin.

What you know

  • Your expertise about classesWhat each program teaches and who it fits.
  • Your experienceAs a Gateway student, professor, or staff member.

The solution

Slate: our shared memory

We don't fix this by asking fewer questions or caring about fewer people. We fix it by giving ourselves a shared memory. Everything you learn when you “just ask” gets written down in one place, so the whole team can see it and act on it. That place is Slate.

What is a CRM?

Customer
Relationship

A system that keeps track of every person you have a relationship with, and every interaction you've had with them. Think of it as a shared notebook for the whole seminary.

How Slate removes the obstacles

Back to our 3 bad things | 4 things that aren't great

  1. 1
    The problemMissing the right peopleYikes!
    With Slate

    Every form, event registration, and inquiry creates or updates a record automatically. If someone raises their hand, they're in Slate, even if nobody on staff talked to them that day.

  2. 2
    The problemForgetting the important detailsEmbarrassing
    With Slate

    Every interaction is saved on the person's record and timeline. You don't have to remember that they want to be a hospital chaplain or that they finish their bachelor's in May. Slate remembers, and so does everyone else who opens that record.

  3. 3
    The problemNot communicating the right informationUnhelpful
    With Slate

    Because we know who they are and what they care about, we can send the right message to the right group at the right time, instead of one generic email to everyone.

  4. 4
    The problemNot seeing the big pictureBlind spot
    With Slate

    Since everyone's data lives in one place, we can count it, filter it, and compare it. Where are inquiries coming from? Which events turn into applications? Which programs are growing? Those patterns show us where to put our effort.
